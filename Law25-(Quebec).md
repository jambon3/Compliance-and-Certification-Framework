# Quebec Law 25 – Personal Information Protection and Privacy Compliance

## 1. Overview

Quebec's **Law 25 (Loi 25)**, formally known as the *Act to modernize legislative provisions as regards the protection of personal information*, introduced significant changes to Quebec's privacy legislation.

Adopted in September 2021, Law 25 modernized the rules governing how organizations collect, use, store, communicate, retain and destroy personal information.

Its primary objective is to strengthen individuals' privacy rights and establish greater accountability for organizations handling personal information.

Law 25 applies to private-sector businesses and organizations operating in Quebec. Public bodies have corresponding obligations under separate legislation.

The legislation requires organizations to implement appropriate administrative, technical and physical safeguards throughout the lifecycle of personal information.

## 2. Applicable Legislation

Law 25 amended two principal Quebec privacy laws:

### Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector

This legislation governs how private-sector organizations handle personal information.

It applies to businesses that collect, hold, use or communicate personal information in the course of their activities.

Examples include:

- Employee records
- Customer contact information
- Payroll and financial information
- Identification documents
- Employment applications
- Medical and benefits information
- Customer account information

### Act respecting Access to documents held by public bodies and the Protection of personal information

This legislation governs access to information and personal information protection within Quebec public bodies.

Examples include:

- Government departments
- Municipal organizations
- Public institutions
- Government agencies

**Important:** Organizations must identify which legislation applies to their operations. Private-sector businesses generally follow the Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector.

## 3. What Is Personal Information?

Personal information is information that relates to a natural person and allows that person to be identified, directly or indirectly.

Examples include:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Identification | Full name, date of birth, address |
| Contact | Email address, telephone number |
| Employment | Employee ID, job title, employment history |
| Financial | Banking information, payroll, salary |
| Medical | Medical records, health and benefits information |
| Digital identity | User accounts, identifiers and certain online activity |
| Authentication | Passwords and authentication information |

Some personal information is considered sensitive because of its nature or the context in which it is used or communicated.

Examples include medical, biometric and other highly private information.

Organizations must apply appropriate protection based on the sensitivity of the information.

## 4. Organizational Responsibilities

Organizations are responsible for protecting personal information throughout its lifecycle.

Law 25 establishes several important responsibilities.

### 4.1 Person Responsible for Personal Information Protection

Every private-sector organization must designate a person responsible for the protection of personal information.

By default, this responsibility belongs to the person exercising the highest authority in the organization.

The role may be delegated in writing, in whole or in part, to another person.

Responsibilities include:

- Overseeing compliance with privacy legislation.
- Establishing privacy governance practices.
- Coordinating privacy-related activities.
- Supporting confidentiality incident management.
- Ensuring privacy policies and procedures are maintained.
- Supporting privacy impact assessments.

The title and contact information of the responsible person must be published on the organization's website or made available through another appropriate means.

### 4.2 Privacy Governance Policies

Organizations must establish and implement policies and practices governing personal information protection.

These should address:

- Collection and use of personal information.
- Access permissions and authorization.
- Information sharing and disclosure.
- Data retention and destruction.
- Employee responsibilities.
- Privacy complaints.
- Confidentiality incident management.
- Training and awareness.

Organizations must make information about their privacy governance policies and practices publicly accessible.

## 5. Collection, Use and Communication of Personal Information

Organizations must have a legitimate purpose for collecting personal information and collect only information necessary for that purpose.

### Collection

When collecting personal information, organizations must provide individuals with clear information about:

- Why the information is being collected.
- How the information will be collected.
- How the information will be used.
- Who may have access to the information.
- Whether the information may be communicated outside Quebec.
- Their applicable access, correction and consent rights.

### Use

Personal information must be used for the purposes for which it was collected, subject to the exceptions permitted by law.

Organizations should restrict access to employees who require the information to perform their responsibilities.

### Communication to Third Parties

Organizations must control how personal information is shared with external parties.

Examples include:

- Cloud service providers.
- Payroll providers.
- Human resources platforms.
- External IT support companies.
- Business partners.
- Data hosting providers.

Contracts and internal procedures should establish appropriate privacy and security responsibilities.

## 6. Privacy Impact Assessments (PIA)

A **Privacy Impact Assessment (PIA)**, known in French as an *Évaluation des facteurs relatifs à la vie privée (ÉFVP)*, is a formal assessment of privacy risks associated with certain projects and activities.

Organizations must conduct PIAs in situations specified by law.

Examples include:

- Developing or acquiring an information system involving personal information.
- Making significant changes to an information system involving personal information.
- Communicating personal information outside Quebec.
- Certain other activities involving the collection, use or communication of personal information.

A PIA should evaluate:

- What personal information is involved.
- Why the information is required.
- Where the information will be stored.
- Who will have access.
- How the information will be protected.
- Whether information will be transferred outside Quebec.
- Potential privacy risks.
- Measures required to reduce identified risks.

### Example: Microsoft 365 Cloud Services

Before implementing a new cloud service that involves personal information, an organization should assess:

1. What personal information will be stored or processed?
2. Where will the information be hosted?
3. Which employees and administrators can access it?
4. Will information be communicated outside Quebec?
5. What security controls are available?
6. What contractual privacy protections are in place?
7. How will information be retained and destroyed?

The assessment must be completed before the applicable project or transfer proceeds.

A PIA is not simply a cybersecurity checklist. It evaluates privacy risks, purposes, data handling and safeguards.

## 7. Technical Security Implementation

IT departments play an important role in implementing the technical safeguards that support privacy compliance.

The following are examples of practical controls.

| Security Area | Implementation | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Identity Management | Active Directory, Microsoft Entra ID | Manage user identities |
| Access Control | RBAC, security groups, access reviews | Restrict access to authorized users |
| Authentication | MFA, Conditional Access | Protect user accounts |
| Endpoint Security | Microsoft Intune, Defender | Protect company devices |
| Encryption | BitLocker, encrypted storage | Protect information against unauthorized access |
| Audit Logging | Microsoft Purview Audit, centralized logging | Record access and administrative activity |
| Data Protection | Microsoft Purview Information Protection | Classify and protect sensitive information |
| Email Security | Defender for Office 365 | Reduce phishing and unauthorized disclosure |
| Document Management | SharePoint permissions and sharing restrictions | Control access to stored documents |
| Data Retention | Retention policies and lifecycle procedures | Manage information throughout its lifecycle |
| Incident Response | Documented response procedures | Investigate and manage privacy incidents |

These technologies support privacy protection, but purchasing or deploying a product does not automatically establish compliance.

Organizations must configure, operate and document their safeguards appropriately.

## 8. Access Control and Employee Management

Organizations should ensure that employees only access personal information required for their responsibilities.

### User Provisioning

When an employee joins an organization:

- Create an individual user account.
- Assign appropriate access permissions.
- Require suitable authentication.
- Provide privacy and security training.
- Document access approvals.

### Role Changes

When an employee changes departments or responsibilities:

- Review existing permissions.
- Remove unnecessary access.
- Assign permissions required for the new role.
- Update access records.

### Employee Offboarding

When an employee leaves the organization:

- Disable the user's account.
- Revoke active sessions and authentication methods.
- Remove group memberships and application access.
- Revoke VPN and remote access.
- Recover company devices.
- Review delegated mailbox and file access.
- Preserve business records according to retention requirements.
- Document the account deactivation and access removal.

Access control should be reviewed periodically and adjusted when responsibilities change.

## 9. Data Retention and Destruction

Organizations must establish retention and destruction rules for personal information.

Personal information should not be retained indefinitely when it is no longer necessary for the purposes for which it was collected, subject to applicable legal retention obligations.

A retention policy should identify:

- Categories of personal information.
- Business and legal retention requirements.
- Retention periods.
- Storage locations.
- Authorized access.
- Destruction procedures.
- Responsibilities for reviewing and enforcing retention.

### Example: Employee Records

An organization may maintain employee records in:

- Human resources platforms.
- Payroll applications.
- SharePoint.
- Exchange Online.
- File servers.
- Backup systems.

The organization should identify where records are stored and establish appropriate retention and destruction procedures for each location.

Deleting a file from a user's computer does not necessarily delete all copies from email, cloud storage, archives or backups.

## 10. Confidentiality Incidents

A confidentiality incident occurs when personal information is:

- Accessed without authorization.
- Used without authorization.
- Communicated without authorization.
- Lost or otherwise subject to a breach of protection.

Examples include:

- An employee sends a payroll spreadsheet to the wrong recipient.
- A company laptop containing personal information is stolen.
- An attacker gains access to an employee's Microsoft 365 account.
- A SharePoint folder containing employee records is accidentally made public.
- A ransomware attack exposes customer information.
- An employee accesses personal information without authorization.

### 10.1 Incident Response Responsibilities

When an organization has reason to believe a confidentiality incident has occurred, it must take reasonable measures to reduce the risk of harm and prevent similar incidents.

The organization should:

1. Identify the incident.
2. Contain the affected systems or accounts.
3. Determine what personal information was involved.
4. Identify the individuals affected.
5. Assess the sensitivity of the information.
6. Evaluate the anticipated consequences and likelihood of harmful use.
7. Consult the person responsible for personal information protection.
8. Implement corrective and preventive measures.
9. Document the investigation and decisions.

### 10.2 Notification Requirements

If an incident presents a risk of serious injury, the organization must promptly notify:

- The Commission d'accès à l'information du Québec (CAI).
- The individuals whose personal information is affected.

The assessment must consider the sensitivity of the information, the anticipated consequences of its use and the likelihood that it will be used for harmful purposes.

### 10.3 Confidentiality Incident Register

Organizations must maintain a register of confidentiality incidents.

The register must include incidents even when they do not present a risk of serious injury.

The register should document:

- Description of the personal information involved.
- Circumstances of the incident.
- Date or estimated period of the incident.
- Date the organization became aware of it.
- Number of affected individuals.
- Assessment of the risk of serious injury.
- Notifications provided, where applicable.
- Corrective measures taken.

The register must be updated and retained for at least five years after the organization becomes aware of the incident.

A copy must be provided to the CAI upon request.

## 11. Individual Privacy Rights

Law 25 strengthens the rights of individuals regarding their personal information.

Depending on the circumstances and applicable legal provisions, individuals may exercise rights including:

### Right of Access

Individuals may request access to personal information held about them.

Organizations must have a process for receiving and responding to these requests.

### Right of Correction

Individuals may request corrections to inaccurate, incomplete or ambiguous personal information.

### Right to Withdraw Consent

Where consent is the basis for collecting, using or communicating personal information, individuals may withdraw their consent, subject to applicable legal requirements.

### Right to Data Portability

Under applicable provisions, individuals may request certain computerized personal information collected from them in a structured, commonly used technological format.

Organizations should establish procedures to receive, authenticate, track and respond to privacy rights requests.

## 12. Privacy Training and Employee Awareness

Employees must understand their responsibilities when handling personal information.

Training should cover:

- Identifying personal and sensitive information.
- Proper handling of employee and customer records.
- Secure document sharing.
- Email security and phishing awareness.
- Password and MFA protection.
- Reporting suspected confidentiality incidents.
- Appropriate use of company systems.
- Privacy responsibilities when working remotely.

Employees with administrative privileges or access to sensitive information may require additional role-specific training.

Organizations should retain evidence of training completion.

Examples include:

## 15. Enforcement and Penalties

The Commission d'accès à l'information du Québec (CAI) is responsible for overseeing compliance with Quebec's privacy legislation.

The legislation provides for administrative monetary penalties and penal sanctions.

For certain violations under the private-sector legislation, administrative monetary penalties can reach the greater of:

- $10 million; or
- 2% of worldwide turnover for the preceding fiscal year.

For certain penal offences, fines can reach the greater of:

- $25 million; or
- 4% of worldwide turnover for the preceding fiscal year.

The applicable amount depends on the nature of the violation, the legal provision and the enforcement process.

## Conclusion

Quebec Law 25 establishes responsibilities for organizations that collect, use, store or communicate personal information.

Compliance requires more than implementing cybersecurity software. Organizations must establish privacy governance, define responsibilities, control access, manage information throughout its lifecycle, assess privacy risks and respond appropriately to confidentiality incidents.

IT departments support these obligations through identity management, access control, encryption, endpoint protection, audit logging, retention management and incident response.

Ultimately, the organization must be able to demonstrate that personal information is handled responsibly and protected throughout its lifecycle.
