## 1. Introduction

**ISO/IEC 27001:2022** is an international standard that specifies
requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and
continually improving an **Information Security Management System
(ISMS)**.

An ISMS is a coordinated management system used to identify information
security risks and apply appropriate measures to protect an
organization's information and supporting systems.

ISO 27001 is not simply an antivirus, firewall, or IT security
configuration standard. It combines:

-   Governance and management accountability
-   Information security risk management
-   Policies and documented procedures
-   People and organizational responsibilities
-   Physical security
-   Technical security controls
-   Monitoring, auditing, and continual improvement

The standard can be applied to organizations of different sizes and
across different industries.

## 2. The Purpose of ISO 27001

The primary objective is to protect information and ensure that security
risks are managed systematically.

An organization implementing ISO 27001 should be able to demonstrate
that it:

1.  Understands its business, information assets, and security
    obligations.
2.  Identifies and evaluates information security risks.
3.  Selects and implements appropriate risk treatments.
4.  Assigns responsibilities and provides adequate resources.
5.  Monitors whether security controls are operating effectively.
6.  Reviews performance and addresses deficiencies.
7.  Continually improves its ISMS.

### 2.1 Scope of an ISMS

The organization defines which business units, locations, processes,
systems, and information are included in the ISMS.

A scope statement should identify:

-   The organization or business units covered
-   Physical locations and operating environments
-   Included information systems and services
-   Relevant internal and external dependencies
-   Interfaces with activities outside the ISMS scope
-   Any justified boundaries or exclusions

A scope that is too narrow can leave important systems and business
processes outside the security management program.

Other important security concepts include:

### 4.1 Clause 4 --- Context of the Organization

The organization must understand the internal and external issues that
affect its information security objectives.

Examples:

-   Regulatory and contractual requirements
-   Customer security requirements
-   Cloud service dependencies
-   Remote and hybrid work arrangements
-   Threat landscape and cyber risks
-   Organizational structure and responsibilities
-   Legacy infrastructure and technical limitations

The organization must identify relevant interested parties, such as
customers, employees, suppliers, regulators, and business partners, and
understand their relevant requirements.

**Implementation evidence:**

-   ISMS scope statement
-   Business and organizational context analysis
-   Interested-party register
-   List of applicable legal, regulatory, and contractual requirements

### 4.2 Clause 5 --- Leadership

Top management must demonstrate commitment to the ISMS.

Management responsibilities include:

-   Approving the information security policy
-   Ensuring security objectives align with business objectives
-   Providing appropriate resources
-   Assigning and communicating security responsibilities
-   Supporting continual improvement

Information security should not be treated as the IT department's
responsibility alone. Business owners and management must participate in
risk decisions.

**Implementation evidence:**

-   Approved information security policy
-   Responsibility matrix
-   Management meeting records
-   Assigned risk owners
-   Evidence of management support and resource allocation

### 4.3 Clause 6 --- Planning

The organization must determine information security risks and
opportunities and establish measurable security objectives.

Planning includes:

1.  Establishing risk assessment criteria.
2.  Identifying information security risks.
3.  Analyzing likelihood and impact.
4.  Evaluating risks against acceptance criteria.
5.  Selecting risk treatment options.
6.  Preparing a risk treatment plan.
7.  Producing and maintaining a Statement of Applicability (SoA).

Security objectives should be measurable where practical, assigned to
owners, monitored, and updated when necessary.

**Implementation evidence:**

-   Risk assessment methodology
-   Risk register
-   Risk treatment plan
-   Statement of Applicability
-   Information security objectives and progress records

### 4.4 Clause 7 --- Support

The organization must provide the resources required to operate the
ISMS.

Key areas:

-   Resources and staffing
-   Competence and training
-   Employee security awareness
-   Internal and external communication
-   Control of documented information

Documentation must be appropriately created, reviewed, approved,
protected, updated, retained, and made available to authorized users.

**Implementation evidence:**

-   Training records
-   Security awareness campaigns
-   Role and competency records
-   Document register
-   Policy approval and revision history

### 4.5 Clause 8 --- Operation

The organization must plan, implement, and control the processes needed
to meet ISMS requirements.

Operational activities include:

-   Performing information security risk assessments at planned
    intervals and when significant changes occur
-   Implementing risk treatment plans
-   Controlling outsourced processes where relevant
-   Retaining documented evidence that processes were carried out as
    planned

**Implementation evidence:**

-   Access reviews
-   Change records
-   Vulnerability remediation records
-   Backup and recovery test results
-   Incident records
-   Risk reassessment records

### 4.6 Clause 9 --- Performance Evaluation

The organization must evaluate whether the ISMS is effective.

Activities include:

-   Defining what must be monitored and measured
-   Evaluating security performance
-   Conducting internal audits
-   Performing management reviews

Useful metrics may include:

-   Percentage of endpoints meeting security baselines
-   MFA coverage
-   Number of overdue critical vulnerabilities
-   Backup job success rate
-   Time to detect and respond to incidents
-   Completion rate of access reviews
-   Security awareness training completion

Metrics should be meaningful, consistently measured, and connected to
security objectives. A high number of completed tasks does not
automatically prove that security is effective.

### 4.7 Clause 10 --- Improvement

The organization must respond to nonconformities and continually improve
the ISMS.

When a nonconformity occurs:

1.  React to the issue and control or correct it.
2.  Evaluate whether action is needed to eliminate its cause.
3.  Determine the root cause.
4.  Implement corrective action.
5.  Review whether the action was effective.
6.  Update the ISMS or risk assessment where necessary.

**Implementation evidence:**

-   Corrective action register
-   Root-cause analysis
-   Audit findings and closure records
-   Lessons learned
-   Updated policies and procedures

## 5. Risk Management

Risk management is central to ISO 27001.

The organization must use a defined and repeatable method to identify,
analyze, evaluate, and treat information security risks.

### 5.1 Identify information assets

An asset can include:

-   Servers and virtual machines
-   Workstations and laptops
-   Mobile devices
-   Network infrastructure
-   Cloud subscriptions and services
-   Applications and databases
-   Source code and intellectual property
-   Customer and employee information
-   Backup repositories
-   Identity and authentication systems
-   Paper records and physical facilities

Each important asset should have an owner and an appropriate
classification.

### 5.2 Identify threats and vulnerabilities

A threat is a potential cause of an unwanted event. A vulnerability is a
weakness that a threat could exploit.

### 5.3 Analyze and evaluate risk

A simple risk model can use likelihood and impact scores.

**Example formula:**

`Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact`

Example scoring:

-   Likelihood: 1--5
-   Impact: 1--5
-   Possible score: 1--25

Illustrative rating:

  Score    Rating
  -------- ----------
  1--4     Low
  5--9     Moderate
  10--16   High
  17--25   Critical

These thresholds are examples only. Each organization must define and
approve its own risk criteria, including acceptance thresholds.

### 5.4 Risk treatment options

Organizations generally choose one or more of the following approaches:

-   **Modify:** Implement controls to reduce likelihood or impact.
-   **Avoid:** Stop the activity that creates the risk.
-   **Share:** Share or transfer some risk through contractual
    arrangements or insurance, while retaining appropriate oversight.
-   **Retain:** Formally accept the risk when justified and approved by
    the appropriate risk owner.

Risk acceptance should be documented and approved by an authorized
person.

## 6. Annex A: The 93 Reference Controls

into four themes.

The organization compares its selected risk treatments with Annex A and
determines which controls are necessary. It documents the applicable
controls, implementation status, and justifications for inclusion or
exclusion in the Statement of Applicability.

Annex A is not a requirement to implement every control regardless of
risk. Controls outside Annex A may also be necessary.

## 7. Technical Implementation Examples

The following examples show how common enterprise technologies can
support selected ISO 27001 controls. Using a product does not
automatically establish compliance; the organization must define
requirements, configure controls appropriately, operate them
consistently, and retain evidence.

**Relevant controls:** A.5.15, A.5.16, A.5.17, A.5.18, A.8.2, A.8.3,
A.8.5

-   Microsoft Entra ID
-   Active Directory
-   Multi-factor authentication (MFA)
-   Role-based access control (RBAC)
-   Privileged Identity Management (PIM), where licensed

## 9. Incident Management

An information security incident is an event that compromises or
threatens the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information
or systems.

Examples:

-   Phishing and credential theft
-   Ransomware
-   Unauthorized access
-   Data leakage
-   Malware infection
-   Denial-of-service attacks
-   Lost or stolen devices
-   Unauthorized changes to infrastructure

### 9.1 Incident response lifecycle

1.  **Preparation:** Establish procedures, roles, tools, and
    communication channels.
2.  **Detection and reporting:** Identify suspicious events and receive
    reports.
3.  **Assessment:** Determine whether an event is an incident and
    evaluate severity.
4.  **Containment:** Limit further impact.
5.  **Eradication:** Remove the cause and malicious artifacts.
6.  **Recovery:** Restore affected services safely.
7.  **Lessons learned:** Document causes, impact, response, and
    improvements.

### 9.2 Incident record

A useful incident record should include:

-   Incident identifier
-   Date and time detected
-   Reporter
-   Affected systems and information
-   Description of the event
-   Severity and business impact
-   Investigation timeline
-   Containment and remediation actions
-   Evidence collected
-   Notifications and escalation
-   Recovery confirmation
-   Root cause and lessons learned
-   Corrective actions and owners

Evidence must be collected and handled carefully, especially when legal,
regulatory, contractual, or disciplinary processes may be involved.

## 10. Business Continuity and Backup

Information security includes preparing for disruptions that affect the
availability of systems and information.

Important concepts:

-   **Business Impact Analysis (BIA):** Identifies critical activities
    and the impact of disruption.
-   **Recovery Time Objective (RTO):** Target time to restore a service
    or process.
-   **Recovery Point Objective (RPO):** Target maximum amount of data
    loss measured in time.
-   **Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP):** Describes how technology services
    are restored.
-   **Business Continuity Plan (BCP):** Describes how essential business
    activities continue during disruption.

A backup is not proven effective simply because a backup job reports
success. Restoration must be tested, and the results should be
documented.

## 11. Internal Audits and Management Reviews

### 11.1 Internal audits

Internal audits evaluate whether the ISMS conforms to the organization's
requirements and ISO 27001 requirements, and whether it is effectively
implemented and maintained.

An audit program should consider:

-   Process importance
-   Changes affecting the organization
-   Results of previous audits
-   Relevant risks
-   Audit scope and criteria
-   Auditor objectivity and impartiality

Auditors should collect objective evidence, document findings, and
communicate results to relevant management.

### 11.2 Management reviews

Top management reviews the ISMS at planned intervals to ensure its
continuing suitability, adequacy, and effectiveness.

Review inputs include:

-   Status of previous review actions
-   Changes in internal and external issues
-   Changes in interested-party requirements
-   Information security performance
-   Nonconformities and corrective actions
-   Monitoring and measurement results
-   Audit results
-   Risk assessment and treatment status
-   Opportunities for continual improvement

Review outputs should include decisions and actions relating to
improvement opportunities and any needed changes to the ISMS.

## 12. ISO 27001 Certification Process

ISO 27001 certification is generally issued to an organization's ISMS
within a defined scope. It is not a personal certification for an IT
administrator.

A typical certification journey:

1.  **Preparation and gap assessment:** Compare current practices with
    the standard.
2.  **Define the ISMS scope:** Identify the systems, processes,
    locations, and business units covered.
3.  **Establish governance:** Assign management responsibilities and
    approve policies.
4.  **Conduct risk assessment:** Identify and evaluate information
    security risks.
5.  **Plan risk treatment:** Select controls and prepare the Statement
    of Applicability.
6.  **Implement controls:** Deploy processes and technical safeguards.
7.  **Collect evidence:** Demonstrate that the ISMS operates as
    intended.
8.  **Conduct internal audit:** Evaluate conformity and effectiveness.
9.  **Conduct management review:** Obtain management evaluation and
    decisions.
10. **Certification audit --- Stage 1:** The certification body reviews
    ISMS documentation and readiness.
11. **Certification audit --- Stage 2:** The certification body
    evaluates implementation and effectiveness.
12. **Address findings:** Resolve nonconformities within the applicable
    certification body's process.
13. **Surveillance and recertification:** Maintain the ISMS through
    ongoing surveillance and periodic recertification.

The certification body, not ISO itself, performs the certification
assessment and issues the certificate.

## 14. Practical Implementation Roadmap

The following is an illustrative implementation sequence. Actual
duration depends on organizational size, scope, existing controls,
complexity, and available resources.

### Phase 1 --- Establish the foundation

-   Obtain management sponsorship.
-   Appoint an ISMS owner.
-   Define the ISMS scope.
-   Identify interested parties and applicable obligations.
-   Identify critical business processes and information assets.
-   Review existing policies and security practices.

### Phase 2 --- Assess risks

-   Establish a risk assessment methodology.
-   Identify threats and vulnerabilities.
-   Evaluate likelihood and impact.
-   Identify existing controls.
-   Determine risk treatment options.
-   Assign risk owners.
-   Record decisions in the risk register.

### Phase 3 --- Select and plan controls

-   Review Annex A against assessed risks.
-   Identify other necessary controls.
-   Prepare the Statement of Applicability.
-   Establish a risk treatment plan.
-   Assign implementation owners and deadlines.
-   Define measurable security objectives.

### Phase 4 --- Implement controls

-   Improve identity and access management.
-   Secure endpoints and servers.
-   Implement network security measures.
-   Establish vulnerability and patch management.
-   Protect information through encryption and classification.
-   Improve logging and monitoring.
-   Implement backup and recovery requirements.
-   Establish supplier and incident management processes.
-   Deliver employee awareness training.

### Phase 5 --- Validate the ISMS

-   Collect operational evidence.
-   Measure control performance.
-   Conduct internal audits.
-   Record and address nonconformities.
-   Perform management reviews.
-   Update risk assessments and treatment plans.

### Phase 6 --- Certification readiness

-   Confirm that the defined ISMS scope is operating.
-   Confirm that required documentation and evidence are available.
-   Resolve outstanding audit findings.
-   Select an appropriate accredited certification body.
-   Complete Stage 1 and Stage 2 certification audits.

## 15. IT Administrator Implementation Checklist

This checklist is a practical starting point for an IT team. It does not
replace a formal risk assessment or the complete requirements of ISO
27001.

### Identity and access

-   [ ] Maintain an accurate user and administrator inventory.
-   [ ] Enforce MFA according to the organization's authentication
    policy.
-   [ ] Apply least-privilege access.
-   [ ] Separate standard and privileged accounts.
-   [ ] Review group memberships and privileged roles.
-   [ ] Remove access promptly when employees leave.
-   [ ] Document access approvals.

### Endpoints and servers

-   [ ] Maintain a hardware and software inventory.
-   [ ] Enforce endpoint encryption where required.
-   [ ] Protect encryption recovery keys.
-   [ ] Deploy endpoint protection.
-   [ ] Maintain secure configuration baselines.
-   [ ] Apply operating system and application updates.
-   [ ] Monitor endpoint compliance.
-   [ ] Restrict local administrator privileges.

### Network and infrastructure

-   [ ] Maintain current network diagrams.
-   [ ] Document VLANs, subnets, and firewall rules.
-   [ ] Restrict network administration access.
-   [ ] Secure remote access.
-   [ ] Review network segmentation.
-   [ ] Back up network device configurations.
-   [ ] Monitor important network events.

### Backup and recovery

-   [ ] Define backup scope and retention.
-   [ ] Document RPO and RTO requirements.
-   [ ] Restrict backup administration.
-   [ ] Protect backup repositories.
-   [ ] Monitor backup job failures.
-   [ ] Perform restoration tests.
-   [ ] Document recovery test results.

### Monitoring and incident response

-   [ ] Identify required log sources.
-   [ ] Configure centralized logging where appropriate.
-   [ ] Protect logs from unauthorized changes.
-   [ ] Establish alert triage procedures.
-   [ ] Maintain an incident register.
-   [ ] Document incident escalation and communications.
-   [ ] Perform lessons-learned reviews.

### Governance and audit readiness

-   [ ] Maintain approved security policies.
-   [ ] Assign asset and risk owners.
-   [ ] Maintain a risk register.
-   [ ] Maintain a Statement of Applicability.
-   [ ] Document supplier security reviews.
-   [ ] Maintain security awareness records.
-   [ ] Track corrective actions.
-   [ ] Conduct internal audits.
-   [ ] Retain evidence of management reviews.
